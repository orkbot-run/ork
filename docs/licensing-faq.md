# Licensing FAQ

The [license map](../LICENSE.md) identifies which material each license covers.
These examples explain the intended policy for PolyForm-covered Ork work.
The actual license and applicable law govern your use.

## Can I self-host for free?

Yes, for the purposes permitted by [PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0](../LICENSE-POLYFORM).
You still pay for your hardware and any services you connect. Running locally
does not by itself make a commercial purpose noncommercial.

## Can a company use it for free?

A company has no general exemption. Using Ork to automate a for-profit business,
including internal administration, engineering, or customer support, requires a
paid license for the PolyForm-covered work unless PolyForm expressly permits
the use or applicable law allows it. Free access to the source does not grant
free use in business operations.

PolyForm permits any noncommercial purpose. A company's noncommercial activity
must fit the actual terms. Ask for a written assessment before
relying on that permission for a borderline activity.

## What about personal projects and research?

A private hobby project or personal study without an anticipated commercial
application fits PolyForm's personal-use permission. Building a prototype for a
planned paid product does not qualify for that particular permission just
because it has no customers yet. Evaluate it under the rest of the license or
obtain a commercial license before use.

## Can a school, charity, or government institution use it?

PolyForm explicitly permits use by charitable organizations, educational
institutions, public research organizations, public safety or health
organizations, environmental protection organizations, and government
institutions, regardless of their funding or resulting funding obligations.
The license does not extend this permission automatically to a contractor
selling services to one of those organizations.

## What requires a commercial agreement?

For PolyForm-covered work, examples include operating bots for a for-profit
business, selling hosted access, embedding Ork in a paid product, and providing
paid automation or consulting with it. The intended purpose matters. Charging
zero for an offering does not establish that its purpose is noncommercial.

## Can I use the original Rakazo code commercially?

Yes, under [Apache 2.0](../LICENSE). That includes the Apache material in the
baseline identified in [LICENSE.md](../LICENSE.md). The new policy does not
withdraw earlier Apache grants, and a commercial Ork agreement does not replace
your rights to that material.

## Can I modify and redistribute Ork?

Follow the license for each part. PolyForm has distribution and modification
permissions with conditions. Keep its terms or URL and required notices with
copies. Preserve Apache attribution and notices for inherited material, and
mark changes to Apache files. A mixed build requires compliance with both sets
of applicable conditions and with third-party licenses.

## Is Ork open source?

Ork combines open source Apache material with source-available PolyForm work.
PolyForm restricts commercial purposes and is not an OSI-approved open source
license. Describe the combined product as source-available and link to its
license map.

## What does a commercial license cost?

Pricing is by individual quotation. The scope can include internal deployment,
hosting for customers, or redistribution, as agreed in writing. The
[commercial terms](commercial-license.md) are a draft for legal review. A
download, inquiry, or contributor signature does not create a commercial license.

Request a private sales channel through a
[licensing inquiry](https://github.com/orkbot-run/ork/issues/new). Keep personal,
customer, and contract details out of the public issue.

## Do contributors keep their copyright?

Yes. The proposed [CLA](cla/v1.md) grants the named Ork licensor rights needed
for public and commercial licensing. It does not transfer copyright ownership
or replace the license on code imported from elsewhere. The CLA is pending
legal review and activation.
